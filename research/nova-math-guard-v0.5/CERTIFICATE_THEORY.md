# Conditional polynomial certificate: meaning, soundness, completeness

## Mathematical statement

Fix a finite set of variables `X` and the commutative polynomial ring `Q[X]`.
Let the hypotheses be `L_i(X)=R_i(X)` for `1<=i<=m` and the goal be `A(X)=B(X)`.
Put `h_i=L_i-R_i` and `t=A-B`. Each multiplier `q_i(X)` is a polynomial with rational coefficients.

The certificate verifies, by exact arithmetic, the identity

    t(X) = q_1(X)*h_1(X) + ... + q_m(X)*h_m(X).

For every assignment `v` over the rational numbers that satisfies each hypothesis,
`h_i(v)=0`, and therefore `t(v)=sum_i q_i(v)*0=0`. Thus `A(v)=B(v)`.
This is a sound proof of the universal implication over Q **assuming the verifier's
implementation of exact polynomial arithmetic is correct**. This algebraic
certificate also implies the identity in any commutative Q-algebra.

## How it is checked

The generator operates on sparse named-variable monomials. An entirely separate
verifier uses fixed exponent vectors and independently parses every given
hypothesis and the goal. It parses the multipliers from strictly canonical
JSON normal forms, recomputes the linear combination, and compares its exact
rational-coefficient normal form against the goal's difference.

An attacker cannot simply change the statement, conclusion, multiplier, or
schema and get a valid certificate (the suite includes mutation tests). This
is a tested engineering property, not a formal proof about the Python code.

## What it cannot do

Ideal membership is sufficient but not necessary for a polynomial implication
over Q: for example, the hypothesis `x**2=0` implies `x=0` over Q, but the
polynomial `x` is not in the ideal generated by `x**2`.

v0.5 uses a bounded Gröbner-basis search (Buchberger completion with graded
lexicographic ordering). For each basis polynomial, it stores a *lifting*
identity to the original hypotheses. S-polynomials and polynomial division
preserve these lifting identities. When the target polynomial reduces to
zero, the accumulated quotients are composed with the lifts to obtain a
certificate in the original hypothesis basis; the independent verifier checks
that final identity. The algorithm's search strategy is not trusted by the
verifier. Completion of Buchberger's algorithm without resource cutoffs is a
classical decision procedure for **ideal membership** in polynomial rings
over Q; our strict limits can turn even ideal members into UNKNOWN.

For example, from `x*y=1` and `y**2=1`, v0.5 proves `x=y` with the
polynomial identity `x-y=y*(x*y-1)-x*(y**2-1)`, which v0.4 could miss.
This is known algebra, not a new discovery.

Do not interpret failure of ideal membership as a logical counterexample.
For example, over Q the implication `x**2=0` => `x=0` is true even
though the conclusion polynomial is outside the ideal `<x**2>`.


The method does not address nonpolynomial functions, uncertainty in real-world
measurement, mathematical limits and quantifiers, or open conjectures.

## Examples

1. From `x=y` conclude `x**2=y**2`: multiplier `x+y`.
2. From `x+y=3` and `x-y=1` conclude `x=2`: multipliers `1/2`, `1/2`.
3. From `x*y=0` and `x*(y-1)=0` conclude `x=0`: multipliers `1`, `-1`.

All identities above are standard known facts. No mathematical novelty is asserted.
